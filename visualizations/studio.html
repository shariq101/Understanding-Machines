Part XVI · the ablation studio

Take the model apart.

One experiment, four ways to look at it. Remove a head, an MLP, or a whole layer; read what happened to the answer; put it back. Nothing runs in the browser — every state you can reach was measured once on a GPU, and every number on this page is a lookup.

The same model before any cut — the activation atlas →

The layer board

What “remove” means here. Replacing a node does not delete it from the graph. The node keeps its shape and is fed the same input, but its write into the residual stream is replaced by the write it produced for a different image. That is the intervention every condition on this page measures. A whole layer is removed the same way: all of its heads and its MLP are replaced at once, which is what the block grid stores.

When the model is too large to run here

The same question, answered in print

Four of the studies below are language models we could not fit on the card that ran the vision sweep. Their papers do publish the numbers, and those numbers are copied here from the tables, not read off a figure. The operation is not identical — most of these delete a layer rather than replace its write — and neither is the task, so compare the shape of the result, not the digits.

BERT-base: keep one head, lose nothing

The change in MNLI-matched accuracy when a layer is reduced to its single best attention head. Twelve layers, twelve tiny numbers.

Michel, Levy & Neubig, Are Sixteen Heads Really Better than One? (NeurIPS 2019), Table 3, arXiv:1905.10650v3. The paper reports none of these changes as significant at p<0.01.

Llama-2-7B: the layer is mostly its FFN

Perplexity on 100 PG19 samples when the last block, or one of its two halves, is taken out. Lower is better; the dense model is the dashed line.

Men et al., ShortGPT: Layers in Large Language Models are More Redundant Than You Expect (Findings of ACL 2025), Table 1, arXiv:2403.03853v3. 7.60 → 13.37 for the whole block; attention alone costs 0.05.

Llama-2-7B: the benchmark notices late

Layers removed in the paper’s own importance order. Perplexity multiplies five times while MMLU barely moves — until the depth runs out.

ShortGPT, Table 4, arXiv:2403.03853v3: Llama-2-7B-Base, 4-bit GPTQ checkpoint. Ratios 0–27.1% of layers; PPL 8.03 → 40.78; MMLU 43.17 → 43.35. The paper’s unquantized rows are in a different table and do not match these, so only this checkpoint’s numbers are drawn.

Where the cliff is, by family

The fraction of layers the authors report removing before accuracy collapses toward chance. These are ranges stated in the text, not a measured series, and there is no per-layer curve to draw.

Gromov et al., The Unreasonable Ineffectiveness of the Deeper Layers, §4, arXiv:2403.17887v2. The paper’s accuracy results are figures, not tables; only the paragraph-level ranges are quoted here.

Why these four. Each one answers a question this page can ask of a vision transformer but cannot ask of a 7-billion-parameter language model on a laptop: does a single head matter, does a layer matter more through its attention or its MLP, and how deep can you cut before the benchmark falls over? The BERT result is the same shape as the head atlas here — single heads are nearly free. The Llama result is the same shape as the MLP column here — the feed-forward block carries most of the layer. Neither agreement was engineered; both are what the measurements say.
