Part XVI · the ripple

Cut one layer. Watch the damage travel.

Loading the measured residuals…

The architecture

click a block to cut it

Inside one block

LN→12 heads→concat · out-proj→+residual
LN→MLP→+residual

The two + marks are where the ablated write enters the stream: a head’s slice of the concatenated write, the MLP’s write, or both for a whole-block cut.

The measured ripple, as numbers

Each bar is measured, not drawn: the relative change in the residual stream at the final token, at that depth, while the selected piece is mean-ablated. Before the cut the bar is empty because nothing has happened yet.

The travel matrix

row = layer cut · column = depth measured

What is being measured. For one prompt, the model runs clean and its residual stream is recorded after each of GPT-2's twelve blocks, at the final token. Then one whole block — all twelve heads plus its MLP — is mean-ablated, the model runs again, and the same twelve points are recorded. The bar at depth d is ‖hdcut − hdclean‖ divided by ‖hdclean‖, so it is a fraction, not a distance in some arbitrary unit. The matrix repeats this for every layer, which is why the area above the diagonal is exactly zero: cutting block 7 cannot change the stream that left block 3. That zero is a check on the measurement, not an assumption.

What the ripple is not. It is not the only path by which a cut reaches the output. Attention reads the whole residual stream, so a change at depth 3 is also read directly by every later block; the bars show where the stream differs, not how much each later block contributed to the final logits. The output panel is the measured end of the chain: KL from the clean distribution, and the clean top-1 token’s probability after the cut.
