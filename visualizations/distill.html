Part XVI · teacher and student

What survives being halved.

BERT-base has twelve blocks; DistilBERT has six, the same width, the same tokenizer, and a training signal that points it at its teacher. This page takes both apart the same way: every block’s attention write, MLP write, two layer norms and every head are replaced by their means over a bank of masked-LM prompts, and the change at the mask is measured. A separate measurement — linear CKA over every token representation — asks which teacher layer each student layer actually resembles. Nothing here runs a model; every number is a lookup.

The same study on a larger pair — RoBERTa-base → DistilRoBERTa, twenty-five prompts, with CKA split into residual, attention and MLP writes — is in the scale check. The 1.5B base→distilled Qwen run is here.

Which teacher layer each student layer resembles

Linear CKA between every student block and every teacher block, over all non-pad token representations in the prompt bank. One means the two organize the same tokens identically up to an orthogonal transform; zero means no linear agreement. The outlined cell in each row is that student layer’s closest teacher layer — the correspondence is measured, not assumed from the 2:1 initialization.

Does the match survive ablation?

CKA is measured on a clean pass; a functional correspondence should also predict where damage lands. For each student block, its twelve whole-block KL values are correlated with each teacher block’s twelve, after removing each layer’s mean and each prompt’s mean so prompt difficulty and layer scale cannot manufacture agreement. This is an intervention fingerprint, not a causal identification: it asks whether two blocks fail on the same prompts, not whether one computes the other.

Which component carries it?

A whole-block fingerprint can hide two different failures: attention may stop routing the same way while the MLP still writes the same feature, or the reverse. This repeats the damage comparison separately for the attention write and the MLP write. It also reports a permutation null: the best of all student–teacher pairs after shuffling the prompt order, so the size of the search is part of the threshold.

Where the answers diverge

Both models complete the same masked prompts. The bar is total variation between the two next-token distributions over the union of their top ten tokens; the words are each model’s own top choice. Small gaps mean the compression preserved the output; large ones show where it did not.

The idea, and what it costs

Distillation replaces a hard label with a distribution. Hinton, Vinyals and Dean (2015) train the student on the teacher’s softened outputs — logits divided by a temperature T — so that the relative probabilities of wrong answers carry information a one-hot label throws away. They call the extra signal dark knowledge: the teacher’s similarity structure over classes, not just its verdict. The student is not fitted to the data alone; it is fitted to the teacher’s function on the data.

DistilBERT (Sanh et al., 2019) is the concrete case measured here. It keeps BERT-base’s width and tokenizer, halves the depth to six blocks, and is initialized by taking every other teacher block. Training then combines three losses: the ordinary masked-LM loss, a distillation loss against the teacher’s softened distribution, and a cosine loss between the student’s and teacher’s hidden states. The paper reports 40% fewer parameters, 60% faster inference, and 97% of BERT-base’s GLUE score. That initialization is why “which layer learned what” is a real question rather than a metaphor: the student starts as a copy of layers 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 and 11, and then moves.

Depth is serial; width is parallel

A transformer block’s compute scales with width times depth, but the two factors buy different things. Width gives each layer more features to write; depth gives the network more sequential transformations. DistilBERT halves depth and keeps width, so each remaining block is as large as its teacher’s, and what is removed is the number of serial steps. If the computation is largely a circuit reused at several depths, halving depth can preserve most of the function. If each layer adds a distinct stage, it cannot. The two stacks on this page are the measured version of that question, block by block, component by component.

What CKA can and cannot say

Linear CKA (Kornblith et al., 2019) compares two representation matrices by the similarity of their Gram matrices. It is invariant to orthogonal transforms and isotropic scaling, which is why it is preferred to raw weight matching, but it is not invariant to arbitrary nonlinear remapping, and a high value does not prove that one layer computes the other’s function. It says the two layers arrange the same inputs similarly in feature space. That is enough to ask whether the student’s six blocks line up monotonically with a subset of the teacher’s twelve, or whether compression reuses a teacher computation at a different depth.

Attention and the MLP are different objects

Attention mixes information across positions; the MLP rewrites each position independently. Prior work finds heads with specialized roles (Voita et al., 2019) and feed-forward layers behaving like key-value memories (Geva et al., 2021). The ablation here separates the concatenated attention write, each individual head, the MLP write, and the two layer norms, so the student’s compression can be read per component instead of as a single block number. A student that preserves attention but loses MLP depth is a different object from one that does the reverse, even when both have six blocks.

What this page measured, and what it did not

The intervention is mean ablation: a component’s write at every token position is replaced by its average over the prompt bank. That removes the component’s input-specific signal while leaving its shape and the rest of the network intact; it is not the same as deleting the block from the graph, and the page says so where it matters. The bank is twelve masked-LM prompts, the readout is the distribution at the mask, and the models are the base checkpoints — no downstream fine-tuning, no instruction tuning, no generation. CKA is measured on the same prompts used for the ablations, so the correspondence and the causal effects are not independent evidence. The claims from the literature above are the papers’ claims, not this run’s; the numbers in the stacks and the matrix are this run’s, and only those.

Reading the two stacks

The bar is the KL divergence at the mask when the whole block — all heads plus the MLP — is mean-ablated, on one shared scale across both models. The three numbers beside it are the separate costs of the attention write, the MLP write, and the average of the two layer norms, each measured on its own. A block whose whole-block cost is close to its larger component is dominated by that component; a block where the whole is much larger than either part is doing something the parts do not do alone. The ripple behind each cut is in the data file and is not redrawn here.

Method

Prompts. Twelve masked-LM sentences, one [MASK] each, tokenized with the shared uncased WordPiece vocabulary. Intervention. Mean ablation at every non-pad token position: the node still runs, but its write is replaced by its mean over the bank. Readout. KL divergence between the clean and ablated distributions at the mask, plus the probability of the clean top-1 and the top five tokens. Components. Concatenated attention write, each head separately, MLP write, post-attention layer norm, post-MLP layer norm, and the whole block (all heads plus MLP). Correspondence. Linear CKA over every non-pad token representation, computed once per layer on the clean pass. Hardware. One T4, float32.

Limits. Encoder-only masked LM, twelve prompts, one intervention family. Mean ablation removes input-specific signal but not the component itself, so a small KL does not mean the block is unnecessary — only that its average write is close to sufficient for these prompts. CKA compares geometry, not computation. None of this is evidence about fine-tuned downstream accuracy, and it should not be read as a compression recipe.
